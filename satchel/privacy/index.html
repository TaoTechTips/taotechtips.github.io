Satchel privacy policy

Effective 27 September 2026

Satchel is an Android app for saving text you reuse and pasting it into other apps. It's made by Taofeek Isiaka-Aliagan ("I" below). This page explains what Satchel does with your information.

In short

  • Your snippets stay on your phone.
  • Satchel has no account, no ads and no analytics.
  • Satchel can't connect to the internet: it doesn't have Android's internet permission.
  • I never receive your snippets or anything else you do in Satchel.

What Satchel stores

The snippets you save (their text, title, folder, tags, whether they're pinned, and when you last used them) and your settings. They're kept in Satchel's private storage on your phone, which other apps can't read.

How text gets into Satchel

Only when you put it there: by typing it, sharing text to Satchel, selecting text and tapping "Satchel", tapping the Save copied widget, or importing a backup file. Satchel doesn't capture anything automatically.

Direct paste (optional)

Direct paste uses Android's accessibility service to put a snippet straight into the text box you're typing in. It's off until you turn it on, and Satchel explains it and asks you first.

When you tap a snippet in the bubble, and only then, Satchel finds the text box you're typing in and reads the text already in it and where your cursor is, so it can put the snippet in the right place. Satchel doesn't save, collect or share that text or anything else on your screen, and it doesn't watch what you type.

You can turn direct paste off at any time in Android's Accessibility settings.

The clipboard

  • When you copy or paste a snippet, Satchel puts it on your clipboard.
  • Satchel reads the clipboard only when you tap the Save copied widget, to save what you copied. Android shows a notice when this happens.
  • If something was copied as sensitive, such as a password from a password manager, Satchel doesn't save it.

What others can see

The bubble and the home-screen widgets show your snippets on screen. Widgets show their titles and the start of their text. Anyone who can see your screen can see them.

Permissions

  • Display over other apps: shows the bubble.
  • Notifications and foreground service: keep the bubble running and show its notification.
  • Accessibility: direct paste, only if you turn it on.

Satchel does not have the internet permission.

Backups

Android's backup. If backup is turned on for your phone's Google account, Android may include Satchel's data (your snippets and settings) in it, and may copy it to a new phone when you set one up. This backup is made by Android and kept in your Google account under Google's terms. On Android 9 and later, when your phone has a screen lock, it's end-to-end encrypted. I can't access it. You can turn it off in your phone's backup settings.

Files you export. Settings → Backup → Export saves your snippets to a file in a place you choose, such as your Downloads folder or Google Drive. You can protect the file with a password. If you forget the password, the file can't be opened, and I can't recover it. A file without a password can be read by anyone who can open it.

Crash reports

If you've allowed it in your phone's settings, Google Play may send me crash and performance reports about Satchel. They describe what went wrong in the app, with details such as the phone model and Android version. They don't contain your snippets or identify you.

Sharing

Satchel doesn't share your information with anyone. There are no third-party services in the app.

Children

Satchel isn't directed at children.

Deleting your data

  • Delete a snippet in Satchel, and it's gone from your phone.
  • Uninstalling Satchel, or clearing its storage in Android's app settings, deletes everything Satchel stored on your phone.
  • Backup files you exported are yours to delete.
  • Android's backup can be deleted in your Google account's backup settings.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, I'll update this page and the date at the top.
